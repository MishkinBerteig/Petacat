# Episodic Evidence and Paper Direction

2026-09-05. Historical-data audit and proposed next study, **not new experimental
results or an amendment to the running `support-v1` protocol**.

## Current Direction

**Large References, Small Checks: Amortized Testing of Stochastic Learning Systems**

The author's clarified objective supersedes the earlier generic diagnostic
title recommendation: expensive oracle construction should enable inexpensive
recurring port checks, and testing the episodic learning mode is central to the
intended paper. The manuscript now uses this title and includes an amortized
cost model, reference-reuse conditions, episode-level formalism, and the
qualified archived learning-mode case study. Good-Turing and p50 are not
presented as newly invented ingredients.

The [novelty review](NOVELTY-REVIEW.md) finds substantial prior art for the
ingredients and related protocols. Its targeted search found no exact complete
pipeline, but cannot establish uniqueness. TMLR explicitly does not require
methodological novelty; supported evidence and findings of interest to its
readership matter more. [TMLR acceptance criteria](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html)

## Qualifications Now Reported

1. **“Convergence” is not what was measured.** The harness chooses the last
   successful answer among eight runs, looking backward past `*NONE*` and
   `*CAP*`. Call it a **last-successful-answer endpoint at horizon eight**.
   Neither stability nor an asymptotic convergence property follows.
2. **The four never-answering episodes are excluded from the old endpoint
   histogram.** They remain counted in the denominator and in a separate field.
   A new study must make the episode outcome total by explicitly representing
   no answer, and preserve whether the last run capped or gave up.
3. **Caps differ.** The saved port episodes use 20,000 codelets per run; the
   historical report describes 100,000 for the reference. Similar aggregate
   cap rates cannot show that this does not change episode behavior. One early
   cap can change the memory and therefore the entire remainder of the episode.
4. **Cap counts in the prose disagree slightly with saved data.** The report's
   later table gives 2,406 capped runs and 1,054 affected episodes; the saved
   post-repair JSON gives 2,404 and 1,052. Use identified artifacts and recomputed
   counts, not an unsupported reconstruction of which intermediate run was used.
5. **A repair is not necessarily an improvement in every summary.** Stored
   novel endpoints decreased, while capped runs increased from 1,311 to 2,404
   in the paired CPU records. Report both. This alone neither proves the repair
   caused every change nor establishes better learning.
6. **The old seed blocks overlap.** All cycles start at 900,000; their episodic
   seeds overlap the single-run check seeds. Before/after measurements are
   paired replay, not independent replications.
7. **Historical prose and console messages retain invalid statistical claims.**
   In particular, recurrence does not rule out finite-reference missing mass;
   `f1/N` is not a certified expected rate under arbitrary port drift; and
   appearing in a single-run reference is not proof of correctness in a learned
   context. Do not copy those statements into the new paper.

Metacat's author explicitly describes memory-dependent duplicate suppression
and warns that resetting a run without clearing memory changes replay behavior.
Those features make memory part of the experimental initial condition, not a
minor runtime option. [Original Metacat documentation](https://science.slc.edu/jmarshall/metacat/)

## Proposed Stateful Extension

### Statistical Unit

Treat a complete episode as **one sampled observation**. Fix the implementation,
parameters, initial memory, input schedule, horizon, codelet caps, and outcome
projection before collecting its construction or validation data. Retain memory
within an episode and reset it between episodes. Check for hidden state leaking
across episodes through replay tests, including replay of later episode seeds
in a fresh process.

If episode `e` produces the ordered trajectory `T_e`, estimate coverage for
`phi(T_e)`, where `phi` is a prespecified projection. `N` in `f1/N` counts
**episodes**, not eight times as many within-episode steps. The draws within an
episode are dependent and may change distribution as memory accumulates.
Repeated, independently randomized reset episodes can instead sample a fixed
episode law under the stated implementation assumptions. This reset-trace
construction already has prior art; it is not a novelty claim.
[Gerhold and Stoelinga, Section 3.1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00165-017-0440-4.pdf)

Continuously growing memory without resets is a different problem. Standard
Good-Turing is not automatically valid for that stream, and stationary Markov
results do not cover arbitrary learning-induced drift.
[Pananjady et al., JMLR 2024](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume25/24-0511/24-0511.pdf)

### Outcomes and Controls

- Primary candidate: `(last successful answer or NO_ANSWER, final-run status)`
  at the fixed horizon. This retains endpoint failures that the historical
  projection hid. Final-run status distinguishes answer, give-up, and cap.
- Always retain all eight ordered states, per-run codelet counts, memory size
  before/after each run, stored-answer identities, and available retrieval/snag
  events. Freeze any secondary position-wise or trajectory summaries in advance;
  a full-sequence support may be too diffuse to estimate usefully.
- Use identical 100,000-codelet per-run caps in reference and port. Never repair
  one capped member of an episode by rerunning it in isolation. Repeat the
  whole assigned episode after infrastructure failure, retaining failed logs.
- Compare **reference versus reference** on held-out episodes for calibration,
  and reference versus port for implementation differences.
- Include matched **memory-retained versus memory-cleared-each-run** conditions
  in both engines. Pair seed schedules within these conditions, and analyze the
  pairing; do not count paired results as independent replications.
- Add a versioned **known memory defect**, such as unintentionally resetting
  memory between steps, alongside a known non-memory defect. A workflow that
  cannot flag the memory-defective control under the declared projection should
  not be described as validating memory dynamics.
- Distinguish repeated-input adaptation from transfer to held-out related
  inputs. Reusing the same analogy eight times can measure memory-conditioned
  exploration; it does not by itself demonstrate useful generalization. A
  learning-performance claim needs an independently justified objective and
  evaluation schedule, not only the engine's internal answer-quality score.

These are proposed controls, not completed experiments. Use a new study ID,
new versioned collectors, and disjoint seed blocks. Run a separate timing and
replay pilot, then freeze a realistic budget before inspecting main outcomes.
Do not append these conditions to the live `support-v1` study.

### Cost Implication

Keeping the current single-run validation target is expensive when the unit
becomes an eight-run episode. With zero discoveries, a per-input one-sided 95%
binomial upper limit below 1e-4 needs at least **29,956 independent episodes**,
or **4,553,312 within-episode runs across 19 inputs**, for validation alone.
This is not a simultaneous 19-input guarantee. A 1e-3 target needs 2,995
episodes per input under the same zero-discovery assumption. Budget and target
must be chosen explicitly; observing nonzero novelty does not permit declaring
that a zero-discovery bound was achieved.

### Measuring Amortization

The paper's central claim needs an evaluation of **upfront reference cost
versus recurring check cost**, not just an unequal sample-size ratio. Freeze
the following accounting and comparisons in the next episodic protocol:

1. Record reference construction, independent validation, artifact preparation,
   port sampling, flag computation, and follow-up separately. Report the
   amortized cost `C_R / K + mean(C_P + C_F)` for a declared number `K` of check
   cycles. An unmeasured component must be labeled unmeasured, not counted as
   zero. Historical development effort cannot be reconstructed from run counts.
2. Report both elapsed campaign time and aggregate execution time, with the
   worker count and runtime environment. Summing concurrent worker times is not
   elapsed campaign time, and neither is automatically CPU time or energy.
   Codelet counts help describe workload but do not equate Scheme and Python
   execution cost. Keep aborted attempts and retries in the accounting.
3. Compare small episode-check budgets using prespecified independent blocks,
   reporting unchanged-reference false alarms and known-defect detection with
   uncertainty. Choose acceptable detection and false-alarm targets before
   using cost to select a method. A cheap check that misses the memory-defective
   control is not evidence of an effective learning-mode regression test.
4. Allow frequency-sensitive and other comparator methods to reuse the same
   reference investment where applicable. Give them the same port-sampling
   budgets and outcome projection, and report their different detection targets.
   Do not manufacture a speed advantage by forcing a comparator to rebuild a
   reference at every revision.
5. Distinguish an observed development history from a projected cost curve.
   Repeated seed blocks on one fixed port are not different port revisions.
   Costs projected for, for example, 10 or 100 revisions are conditional
   scenarios until that many real check cycles are measured. Reuse stops when
   the intended reference behavior or protocol changes; reference reconstruction
   then becomes another upfront cost.

The unchanged `support-v1` collector already records per-run elapsed seconds,
codelets, and attempt start/end times, including failed-attempt durations.
Its analyzer retains timing summaries for construction, validation, and port
phases. These will support descriptive cost accounting once the study is
complete, but they do not supply human investigation time, memory-mode costs,
or a measured history of repeated port revisions. This is a reading of the
[collector](../studies/support-v1/collect.py) and
[analyzer](../studies/support-v1/analyze.py), not a new experimental result or
an amendment to their frozen analysis plan.

## Work We Can Do Before the Current Study Finishes

1. Agree on the working title and frame the contribution around reproducible
   implementation diagnostics, not uniqueness or correctness certification.
2. Expand related work with the closest methods identified in the novelty review.
3. Draft the episode-level formalism and outcome definitions, keeping historical
   evidence separate from the proposed versioned study.
4. Plan head-mass sensitivity (for example 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%) and outcome
   projection ablations. The current protocol fixes p50; any added analysis
   must be labeled as an amendment or exploratory, not retrospectively called
   part of the initial protocol. No superiority of p50 is currently supported.
5. Design known-defect controls and an anonymous artifact release checklist.
   Preserve raw data, quantify diagnostic yield and missed defects, and include
   construction costs instead of presenting only short check costs.
